This page keeps a dated record of what the app looked like at each point, so we can see how the design moved and why. The newest entry goes at the top.

The two entries from 12 November are mock-ups. From 13 November the app runs, and the entries describe what was built. Nothing here is final.

## 13 November 2026, the first built screens

The workout screen went from mock-up to a running app, and the screens around it followed in the same day: bottom nav, profile, history, the settings line, the exercise page and the Stats tab. This entry records each pass and why it landed where it did. There are no screenshots for it yet. Debug builds have launch flags for them, such as `-openProfile`, `-openHistory`, `-editSettings`, `-openExercise` and `-openStats`, and they go in `apps/docs/public/design/2026-11-13/` when taken.

### Bottom nav and the running workout bar

- Three tabs along the bottom: Workout, Stats and Profile. A hairline above them, one icon and a small label each, and the selected tab fills its icon. Profile had been a sheet; a tab is one tap from anywhere.
- Every tab stays alive when you switch, so each keeps its scroll position.
- While a workout runs, a bar sits above the nav with its name, how long it has been going and sets done. Tap it to go back in. The chevron on the workout screen minimises into that bar, so you can check the Stats tab mid-workout without losing the timer.
- The Workout tab is "Start an empty workout" at the top, your prebuilt workouts below it, and "Create new workout" at the bottom.

### Profile

- The profile is about you and your training: photo, name, "Lifting since" with your workout count, and your history. Account things moved behind a gear at the top right, into Settings.
- Settings is name and email, weight unit, theme, sign out and delete account. Each is one row with a hairline under it, matching the rest of the app.
- Tapping the photo picks one from Photos. It is kept on the phone as a small JPEG, and signing out removes it.

### History

- History rows read the way Hevy lists a workout: the name, then "Sat, 7 Nov · 1 h 14 min · 16 sets · 12,705 kg", then the exercises with their set count in front. After five exercises the row says "… 2 more". Volume only counts rep sets, because a second of a hold is not a rep.
- Tapping a row opens the workout with every set as "55 × 8 @8.5" under its setup heading. The menu has Edit sets, Edit name and description, Save as prebuilt workout and Delete workout.
- Editing a past workout reuses the workout screen. The header shows the date instead of a timer, and ticking does not start rest.

### The settings line

- A row per setting took too much room on every exercise, and a bright "Yes" pulled more attention than a setting deserves. Settings are now one quiet line under the exercise name, "Seat 4 · Notch 7 · Dead stop", with a small pencil. A yes or no that is off reads "No dead stop".
- Tap the line and it opens into editable rows, with Add setting and Done underneath. Done drops empty rows and closes it.
- The "+ Setting" button went, so the controls under the name are back to rest, note and brand. Adding a setting lives in the exercise's menu and in the opened rows.
- Group headings are unchanged. They still only show what a group changes, such as "Hold · Notch 8".

### The exercise page

- Tapping an exercise's name, mid-workout or in a past workout, opens its page as a half-height sheet you can drag up. Mid-workout you want to check what you did last time on a machine without leaving the workout.
- It opens on the brand you are on. If you have used more than one brand, chips at the top switch between them. Brands are never mixed.
- Four metrics as chips that scroll sideways and fade at the right edge: Set volume, Session volume, Weight, Est. 1RM. The two volumes have a Weighted / Raw switch, and every number says "weighted" or "kg" next to it so it is never a guess which one you are reading.
- Split by draws a line per value of one setting, such as Notch 7 and Notch 8. The split is remembered per exercise.
- Records sit under the graph in plain kilograms, with the weighted best under the two volumes. Then every past set by date.
- With only one workout, the page says the line starts with the next one instead of drawing a single dot.

### The Stats tab

- History is one workout at a time, so nothing showed whether training is going anywhere. The Stats tab is this week against last, weekly volume and a trend for every exercise.
- This week: workouts, sets and volume as large numbers, with last week's under each.
- Weekly volume: a bar per week for the last 12 weeks. This week is in the accent colour.
- Exercises: everything you have done, most recent first, with its most used brand, when you last did it, a small line of best set volume over the last 8 workouts, and the change across them such as "+10%". Tapping a row opens the exercise page.

### Still undecided

- Which mode is the main look. Both are built, and the theme row in Settings offers system, light and dark.
- Filters on the exercise page, such as pinning a second setting while split, and tapping a point to adjust its factors.
- A Live Activity on the Lock Screen for the rest timer. The rest notification does the job for now.

## 12 November 2026, light mode and a calmer workout screen

We wanted the app to feel more refined, closer to the warm, minimal websites that win design awards. This entry is the second pass. It adds a light mode and works through what made the workout screen feel busy. The numbered marks on the earlier screenshots point at the problem each step fixed.

### What we settled on

- One font for the whole app, Inter. Mixing a serif with a sans looked unsteady.
- A warm beige light mode (`#ECE8E1`) with near-black text, and a dark mode that swaps the same colours: warm near-black with warm off-white text. Both share one layout.
- Thin full-width lines between rows instead of filled blocks. This came from a reference site Anton liked, where a list of services was just large text with a line under each item.
- Weight, reps and RPE sit in soft filled cells, all the same size. The cells separate the numbers and look tappable without any lines between them.
- A faint grain over the screen. It is lighter in dark mode, where grain shows more.
- Big numbers, such as the rest timer and best set, use Inter Light with tighter letter spacing.
- The rest bar sits flat at the bottom behind a thin line instead of floating.

### What we changed, and why

**1. The dot on the brand control.**

<img src="/design/2026-11-12-light/01-brand-dot.png" alt="Brand control showing Technogym with a small sand-coloured dot before it, marked 1" style={{ maxWidth: 420 }} />

1. A coloured dot before the brand name meant nothing and looked like a generated-app habit. We removed it.

**2. Numbers at different sizes.**

<img src="/design/2026-11-12-light/02-number-sizes.png" alt="Set rows where the RPE column, marked 1, is noticeably smaller than the weight and reps beside it" style={{ maxWidth: 300 }} />

1. RPE was smaller than weight and reps, so the row looked uneven. All three are now the same size and weight.

**3. No depth.** With only lines, every row looked the same and nothing told one exercise apart from the next. We tried two fixes, and kept the second:

- Cards. Each exercise sat on a slightly raised panel. It worked, but made the screen feel like a stack of boxes again.
- Soft cells behind the numbers on a flat background. This gives each row something to hold onto without boxing the whole exercise.

**4. A cluttered workout screen.**

<img src="/design/2026-11-12-light/03-cluttered.png" alt="Dark workout screen where the settings line under each set is marked 1, 2 and 3" style={{ maxWidth: 300 }} />

1. The settings for a set (hold, notch 8) sat on a second line under its numbers, crowded against "Previous".
2. The next set started its own settings line.
3. The third set repeated the same settings as the second, word for word.

We first tried removing the outlined rest, note and brand boxes and showing settings only when they changed. Anton preferred the boxes, so they came back. What fixed it was moving the settings out of the set rows:

- "Previous" now shows numbers only, as weight × reps @ RPE, for example `55 × 8 @8.5`.
- Sets that share the same setup are grouped under a small heading, such as "Hold · Notch 8" or "Reps · Notch 7 · Dead stop". A new heading only appears when the setup changes, so an exercise without settings has none.
- Rows have more room: wider side margins, taller sets and more space between exercises.

### Still undecided

- Which mode is the main look. The light mode is closer to the feel we want.
- Whether a group of holds should label its column "Time" instead of "Reps".
- The brand, set fields and reorder screens have not had this pass yet, apart from choosing a brand in light mode.

## 12 November 2026, first direction

The first set of screens we were happy enough with to write down.

### What we settled on

- Black and greys, with an off-white primary button and one muted accent. The accent only marks completed sets, the selected machine and new records.
- One typeface in light and regular weights, in sentence case.
- A title or an action on its own. No small label sitting above every value.
- Corners with a little rounding. Controls are 8 and panels are 12.
- The workout is one scrolling list. Each exercise has rest time, note and machine controls under its name.

### What we tried and dropped

- A neon accent, then an orange one. Both pulled too much attention.
- Condensed uppercase headings with monospace labels. It looked loud, and we want the app to feel calm.
- Fully round pill buttons. They felt too soft.

### How this entry handles flexible sets

- A timed hold shows its duration where a rep set shows reps.
- Your own fields sit on a second line under each set, so the columns stay the same however many fields an exercise has.
- The set fields screen is where you add a field, say whether a higher or lower number is a record, and choose whether each value gets its own progress.
- The exercise history screen has a second row of tabs for those values. Picking "Notch 7" shows the records, graph and sessions for notch 7 only.

### Still undecided

- The typeface. The mock-ups use Hanken Grotesk. The other option is the iOS system font.
- The accent colour. The mock-ups use a muted sand.
