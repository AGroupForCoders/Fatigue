These are the requirements for the [first version](/docs/mvp). They describe behaviour, not screens, so they stay true while the design changes.

## Logging a workout

This is the screen people spend their time on, so it gets the most detail.

- A workout is one scrolling list of exercises. There is no separate "up next" panel.
- You can start a workout empty or from a routine, and add exercises at any point.
- You can reorder exercises by holding an exercise name and dragging it.
- Each exercise has three controls under its name. They are the rest time, a note and the brand.
- By default each set records weight and reps. [What a set records](#what-a-set-records) covers holds, unloaded sets and fields.
- RPE is optional on any set and runs from 6 to 10 in half steps.
- Each set shows what you did last time on the same brand, so you can see what to beat without leaving the screen.
- Ticking a set as done starts the rest timer for that exercise. You can add or remove 15 seconds, or skip it.
- Finishing a workout saves it to your history.

## Exercises

- The app ships with a built-in list of exercises, each with a primary muscle and an equipment type.
- If a search finds nothing, the first result offers to create an exercise with the text you typed.
- A custom exercise needs a name, a primary muscle and an equipment type. Secondary muscles are optional.
- There is no limit on custom exercises. They are private to your account.
- Custom exercises behave exactly like built-in ones. They get brands, fields, history and records.

## Brands

A brand is who made the equipment, such as Technogym or Hammer Strength. "Chest Press" is the exercise. Technogym chest press and Hammer Strength chest press are the same exercise on two brands, and 80 kg on one is not 80 kg on the other.

- Every exercise you log has a brand, including free weights such as dumbbell flies. You can type anything, for example "Gym dumbbells".
- A brand can have an optional free-text label, for example the model or which gym it is in.
- Brands come from a built-in list, and you can type your own.
- When you add an exercise to a workout, it defaults to the brand you used last time, so you only pick one the first time.
- History and records are kept per brand. The exercise page can also show all brands together.

## What a set records

Not every set is weight and reps. We want to progress on range of motion and on time as well, so what a set records has to be flexible.

Take the adductors. One set might be a hold at notch 5 for 30 seconds with no weight. The next might be 50 kg for 5 reps at notch 4. Both belong to the same exercise in the same workout.

- Each set is either a rep set or a hold. A rep set records reps. A hold records a duration.
- Weight is optional on both. A set with no weight is unloaded, which is not the same as 0 kg and never counts toward a weight record.
- One exercise can mix rep sets and holds in the same workout.
- A new exercise has no fields. You add them as you go. A field is either a number, such as "Notch", or a yes or no, such as "Dead stop".
- Fields are logged on every set and filled in from last time. A seat setting that never changes costs nothing to keep, and one you are working on shows up in your history.
- Fields belong to the exercise on one brand. Notch 7 on the Technogym adductors means nothing on the Hammer Strength ones, so they do not carry over.
- A number field can be tracked. You say whether a higher or a lower number is better and give its lowest and highest value, such as notch 1 to 10. The app then detects records on it the same way it does for weight and gives it a line on the graph. Range of motion is almost always a tracked field.
- A field can have its own progress for each value. With this on for "Notch", notch 7 and notch 8 each get their own records and their own line on the graph. It is off unless you turn it on, so a seat height that never changes does not split your history.
- The free-text note stays for anything that does not fit a field.

## Score

Every set gets a score, so one number shows whether you beat last time even when the progress was a deeper notch rather than more weight. [ADR 0001](https://github.com/AGroupForCoders/Fatigue/blob/main/docs/adr/0001-combined-set-score.md) records why.

- The load is weight times reps for a rep set and weight times seconds for a hold. An unloaded set counts its weight as 1.
- Each tracked field multiplies the load by a factor from 1.0 at the worst end of its range to 2.0 at the best end. Yes or no fields and untracked numbers are left out.
- For example, on a notch 1 to 10 field where lower is better, 50 kg for 5 reps scores 389 at notch 5 and 417 at notch 4.
- Scores are only compared between sets of the same shape on the same exercise and brand. The shape is whether it is a rep set or a hold, whether it is loaded, and which tracked fields it has. A hold is never ranked against a rep set.
- Adding a tracked field to an exercise you already have history for starts a new shape. Old sets keep their records and are not compared with the new ones.
- Scores are worked out from the logged values whenever they are shown. Fixing a field's range or direction recalculates every past score.
- The raw values and their own records are always shown next to the score.

## Routines

- A routine is a named, ordered list of exercises, each with its brand, rest time and planned sets.
- You can create, edit and delete routines.
- Starting a workout from a routine copies it, so changes during the workout do not alter the routine.

## History

- You can see a list of past workouts and open any of them.
- Each exercise has a page with its best set, an estimated one-rep max, a progression line and recent sessions.
- The page can be narrowed to one brand, and then to one value of a field that has its own progress, such as notch 7.
- The progression line plots one thing at a time. That can be top set weight, estimated one-rep max, hold time, score or a tracked field such as notch.
- Records are detected when a workout is saved and marked on the session where they happened.

## Account and sync

- Signing in with an email and password is required before you can use the app.
- Everything you log is saved on the phone first, then synced to the server in the background.
- The app works without a connection. Changes made offline sync when the connection returns.
- Signing in on a second device brings down all of your data.
- You can delete your account from inside the app, which removes your data from the server.

## Hevy import

- You can import a Hevy CSV export.
- Imported exercises are matched to the built-in list by name. Anything unmatched becomes a custom exercise.
- Hevy does not record brands, so the import lists each exercise it found and asks you to pick a brand before it finishes.

## How it should feel

- Logging a set never waits on the network.
- The app opens straight into your current workout if one is running.
